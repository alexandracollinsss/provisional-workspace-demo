  • Fictional demo: Northfield Bio semaglutide depot gel

    Component · Whole invention · review: awaiting

    What it does: Semaglutide must be injected weekly; a depot that releases it over three weeks would reduce the injection burden.

    • thermo-responsive gel carrier

      Component · Essential · review: accepted

      What it does: Liquid in the syringe, sets into a depot at body temperature

      Exact version tested: 18% w/w poloxamer 407 with 0.5% w/w sodium hyaluronate and 0.1 mM zinc chloride (F3)

      “18% w/w poloxamer 407, 0.5% w/w sodium hyaluronate, 0.1 mM zinc chloride, 2 mg/mL semaglutide in phosphate buffer pH 7.4”
      — Research update slides (fictional), Slide 3 · open document
      “F3 | 18% w/w | 0.5% w/w sodium hyaluronate | 0.1 mM | 32 °C | yes | 9%”
      — Formulation screening table (fictional) · open document
      “F6 (alginate in place of hyaluronate) released 14% in the first 24 hours, so other anionic polysaccharides may also work, but F6 was not tested in animals.”
      — Experimental report ER-07 (fictional) · open document

      How broadly to describe it (currently: “any thermo-responsive polymer carrier”):

      1. “18% w/w poloxamer 407 with 0.5% w/w sodium hyaluronate and 0.1 mM zinc chloride” — support: supported. Formulation F3, tested in vitro and in rats.
      2. “a poloxamer 407 gel containing an anionic polysaccharide” — support: partial. Hyaluronate (F3) and alginate (F6) were made; only F3 was tested in animals. Ask the inventors what else was tried.
      3. [chosen] “any thermo-responsive polymer carrier” — support: unsupported. Only poloxamer 407 was made; nothing in the packet supports other thermo-responsive polymers.

      Question for the inventors: Besides hyaluronate and alginate, which polymers or polysaccharides were tried, and did any fail? (Broader wording for the carrier needs evidence beyond F3 and F6.)
      Unanswered. Answer it in the interactive app.

    • preparing cold and adding drug last

      Process step · Essential · review: accepted

      What it does: Avoids cloudiness and keeps the peptide intact

      Exact version tested: Polymer dissolved at 4 °C overnight; semaglutide added last below 8 °C

      “Add zinc chloride, then add semaglutide last while keeping the mixture below 8 °C.”
      — Preparation protocol PP-03 (fictional) · open document

      Question for the inventors: Why is semaglutide added last? (The order of steps may be part of the invention.)
      Answer: Fictional inventor answer: adding semaglutide before the polymer fully dissolved made two early batches cloudy, so we always add it last and keep the mix below 8 °C.
